## The wedge (one line, say it everywhere)

**"Own the AI you're renting — a private model, with a receipt that could fail, that anyone can re-run."**

- Not "owned/local models" — that lane is crowded (Ollama, NIM, vLLM). Ownership is table stakes.
- Not "signed receipts" alone — a software-key signature is Level 0 (the holder can fabricate it).
- The moat is **adversarial proof + third-party re-run**: the witness gate proves the test *could fail*, and a GitHub Actions run is a re-execution by someone who doesn't hold your key. Nobody else ships that. Line: *"TEEs prove where it ran. We prove the test could fail — and anyone can re-run it."*

**The buyer, first:** lean AI-native SME / eng team — founder-led, fast decision, real recurring token spend. Not the regulated enterprise (RFP-gated, wants certs you don't hold yet, and its AI-Act urgency slipped to Dec 2027). The government is a separate, bigger, parallel track (below).

**Sell one done-for-you mint. Get one invoice paid.** The product is already live and gated — the only work left is sales.

- Drive the qualifying question: *"What task do you feed an LLM most, and how many times a month?"*

That single paid invoice is the whole game right now — 1,700 repos and £0 because the number that never moved is "humans who paid," not "things built."

## THE LADDER (4 tiers, reframed through the wedge, priced)

### Tier 1 — Mint — the first-revenue engine
The self-serve funnel + the paid done-for-you.
- **Self-serve DIY — free.** The live `fallforgemint`: paste a task + examples → own a private Modelfile, prove it beats base, name it, download a one-file installer, get a signed scorecard. This is the lead magnet and the unfakeable demo.
- **Done-for-you, per model — £2,500–£6,000.** Real weight fine-tune (not few-shot) on local metal, curated examples, held-out proof, signed scorecard, installer, 30-day beats-or-refund.
- **Multi-domain pack (3–5 models) — £9,000–£20,000.**
- **Keep-it-sharp add-on — £300–£900/mo.** Re-mint on drift, fold new examples, fresh scorecard.
- **Assets:** fallforgemint (LIVE, witness 192/193), fallforge-store (Stripe+host to go live), mint-a-node / fallforge/mint.html.
- **Build needed:** none for self-serve. The paid tier is manual fine-tune delivery. Flip the store live for card checkout.

### Tier 2 — Foundry Build — "Proof-Installed" sovereign stack (high-ticket pilot)
Not "we install Ollama" (commodity). **"We install a private, mutation-gated, receipt-emitting AI stack on your metal — every organ proves it works and could fail, and your people can re-run the proof."**
- **Foundry Lite (software-only, their hardware) — £6,000–£12,000 setup.** The stack (fallbrain conductor + minted models + agent-proof audit ledger + witness gate) deployed air-gapped, runbook, 2 seats trained.
- **Foundry Standard (turnkey box + stack) — £15,000–£30,000.** Workstation-class GPU box spec'd + supplied, stack installed air-gapped, 1-day setup, 5 seats, 90-day support.
- **Foundry Rack (department / multi-user) — £35,000–£75,000+.** Server GPU, multi-seat, the full running-company shape (fallbrain doors + signed ledger).
- **The CFO lever (state it straight):** the hardware + setup is qualifying plant & machinery. Under UK **Full Expensing**, a company deducts **100% in year one** on new main-rate plant & machinery — and that explicitly includes IT equipment and software, uncapped, now permanent. (The £1m **Annual Investment Allowance** does the same for companies and sole traders; a new **40% first-year allowance** exists from Jan 2026 for anything that falls outside full expensing.) At 25% corporation tax a **£20,000 foundry is ~£5,000 straight off the tax bill — net ~£15,000, written off this year, not depreciated over five.** That is the finance director's "yes."
- **Assets:** fallbrain, fallforgemint, agent-proof, witness, and the `how-a-company-runs-on-fallforge.pdf` architecture explainer (already built) as the sales doc.
- **Build needed:** package the installer + runbook + a hardware spec sheet.

### Tier 3 — Sovereign Care — the retainer (the real business, the MRR)
Red Hat model: never charge for the MIT code — charge for keeping it **alive, proven, and accountable**.
- **Care Basic — £750–£1,500/mo.** Monthly witness re-run drift audit + a signed drift receipt, patching, re-mint-on-drift, support.
- **Care Standard — £2,000–£4,000/mo.** + governance pack (audit ledger maintained, a quarterly proof-pack for their board/auditor), priority support, model refresh.
- **Care Sovereign — £5,000–£9,000/mo.** + a dedicated sididy ops agent on their metal, continuous drift monitoring, SLA response, assigned engineer.
- **The hook:** *provable AI as a subscription* — every month, a signed receipt that their AI still works, still beats baseline, still hasn't drifted. Nobody else sells that.
- **Assets:** witness (the re-run gate), agent-proof (the ledger), sididy mesh + research-loop (the ops layer, runs free on local), kar-foldcycle (the run-cheap proof: 95% prefill / 67% embed measured).
- **Build needed:** the drift-audit runbook + the monthly proof-pack template.

### Tier 4 — Kard Marketplace / boot keys — the platform play (later, sequenced)
Tamper-evident boot keys (fallkard), curated skins, a marketplace of pre-minted proven organs with rev-share. This is the Series-A-shaped platform — it needs a backend, accounts, and the money rail. Ships **after** Tiers 1–3 have paying customers; opening it first is how the last 1,700 repos happened. Park it, don't kill it.

## Positioning notes that are method, not hedging

- **Pitch the calculable threshold, not a blanket multiple.** The own-vs-rent calculator gives *this* buyer's real number and honestly returns rent-wins below ~500k–2M tokens/day — that honesty is the conversion asset, not a weakness.
- **Sit in the seam nobody occupies.** Two crowded lanes: local inference tooling (Ollama/NIM/vLLM — no proof) and AI-governance/GRC (Credo/Holistic/Fiddler/IBM/Purview — cloud paperwork, no runtime receipt). Sovereign local execution **+** a verifiable per-inference receipt is unoccupied.
- **Konomi credit ships on every product surface** (Thomas Frumkin), and the code stays MIT — you sell the friction, the liability, and the minting, never the code.
